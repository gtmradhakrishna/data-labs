The labs have read the internet. They haven't seen how freight actually moves.

AI models learned from public text. What they can't learn from the web is how a quote gets priced, a claim gets resolved or a shipment gets rescheduled. That knowledge sits in your inbox, your shared drives and your operating systems.

So the labs have started paying for it. Google's winning bid for Spirit Airlines' records was $10 million, according to a federal court filing. Firms a fraction of that size have reportedly been paid in the hundreds of thousands.

The limits you should know

  • No de-identification method removes every risk.
  • If you agree to an optional sample, Pewter or its licensing partner may see it before it's de-identified. You choose what goes in, and you can say no.
  • Holding records doesn't always mean you can license them. Customer and carrier contracts may restrict sharing, so have your lawyer check them.
  • Pewter holds no security certification of its own, such as SOC 2. Extra protections, such as escrow, are negotiated into the agreement.

Before you ask

What owners ask first

Is this real money, or a pitch?

Pewter's licensing partner reports paying $350,000 to $650,000 to firms of 60 to 100 people, and Google's $10 million bid for Spirit Airlines' records appears in a court filing. Those figures come from the publishers' own statements and haven't been independently verified. You'd see a written offer before committing, and many firms are offered less.

Who does what?

Pewter sizes your records, brings you the offer and coordinates the deal through payment. The licensing partner makes the offer, signs the license with you and pays you. Pewter isn't your lawyer or agent, and it isn't a law firm or registered broker-dealer, so have your own lawyer review the agreement.

Who will use the data, and for what?

Authorized AI developers, for training and evaluating models, as defined in your agreement. Exclusivity and permitted uses are written into the terms you approve. If you want to know who the buyer is, or whether the data could help train a competitor's tools, ask on the call and get the answer in the terms.

Will my customers, carriers or employees be exposed?

Names, contact details, account numbers, credentials and payment details are removed or replaced before delivery. No method removes every risk, so you review the process and a sample before signing. You can also ask to exclude pay and HR records in writing.

Who sees my records before I sign?

No one, unless you agree. Pewter may ask to review a small sample you choose, to check quality, and you can decline. The full export happens only after you approve the terms in writing.

How much of my team's time does it take?

A few hours: 2 or 3 calls of about 30 minutes each, plus a short inventory of your systems. If an export is needed, your IT person or provider runs it.

What if I don't know what data we have?

That's common. Tell Pewter which tools you've used over the years, such as email, shared drives and your TMS, and they'll work out what's there on the call.

Does this make sense if we've closed or are winding down?

It can. Closed firms can qualify as long as the records still exist. When subscriptions lapse, archives can get deleted and that value is lost, so check before you cancel any old systems.
