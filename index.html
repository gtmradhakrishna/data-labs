Questions

What owners ask first.

Is this real money?

Pewter's licensing partner reports paying $350,000 to $650,000 to firms of 60 to 100 people, and Google's $10 million winning bid for Spirit Airlines' records appears in a court filing. These figures haven't been independently verified. You'd see a written offer before committing, and many firms are offered less.

Who does what?

Pewter sizes your records, brings you the offer and coordinates the deal. The licensing partner makes the offer, signs the license with you and pays you. Pewter isn't your lawyer or agent, and it isn't a law firm or registered broker-dealer.

Do I lose my data?

No. You license an agreed, de-identified copy. Your originals and your ownership of them don't change.

Who sees my records before I sign?

No one, unless you agree. Pewter may ask to review a small sample you choose, and you can say no. If you agree, Pewter or its licensing partner may see it before it's de-identified. The full export happens only after you approve the terms in writing.

Who will use the data?

Authorized AI developers, for training and evaluating models, as defined in your agreement. Exclusivity and permitted uses are written into the terms you approve, so ask about competitors on the call.

Can I share customer and carrier records?

Holding records doesn't always mean you can license them, and contracts may restrict sharing. Names and contact details are removed, and you can exclude third-party records, though excluding more may lower the price. Have your lawyer check.

How secure is it?

Pewter holds no security certification of its own, such as SOC 2. Extra protections, such as escrow, are negotiated into your agreement.
